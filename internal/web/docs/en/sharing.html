Shared vaults and groups

Besides the personal vault, PassZit has shared vaults for teams: the router passwords for the IT team, the social media accounts for marketing. Sharing keeps the end-to-end encryption — the server never gets the key of a shared vault.

Making a shared vault

The list on the left starts with My vault, followed by the Team vaults by name. The + next to Team vaults makes a new one; you manage it, and it appears in that list. The gear next to its name opens Members and settings. Items are created in the vault shown on the left, or chosen in the item editor under Vault; changing that field moves an existing item (with its files) to another vault.

The policy can allow only administrators to make shared vaults (Settings → Vault policy → Sharing and export).

Rights

The rights of each person and group are check boxes in the vault's settings (Members and settings). A higher right includes the lower ones: ticking one ticks those before it, clearing one clears those after it.

Check boxMay
Connect and fillopen connections (also from WatchZit), fill and copy logins (web vault and extension), but not show passwords, notes, documentation and files — every member has it
See passwordssee everything
Add and changealso add, change and delete items
Managealso members, groups, the name and the vault itself

For example: the group Support gets Connect and fill and See passwords, the group Administrators also Add and change.

The server refuses changes without the right. Connect and fill without See passwords is a convenience, not a lock: whoever can fill a password into a page can find it out with some effort. A vault always keeps at least one manager.

Nobody can change their own access — another manager does it — so temporary rights from a request cannot be made permanent by their holder. A vault always keeps at least one permanent manager (not one with an end date or temporary rights); removing, disabling or deleting the last one is refused.

Adding people

Under Add a person choose the user and the right, optionally an end date. Your browser seals the vault key to the person's public key; the server only stores the sealed key.

The first time, PassZit shows the fingerprint of that key. The person sees the same fingerprint under My account → Your key for sharing; comparing it once (in person or by phone) makes sure nobody put another key in between. PassZit remembers confirmed keys (sealed with your account key) and warns when a person's key changes.

Time-limited access

With an end date the access ends by itself at the end of that day, for example for an external technician. The audit log records the end.

Access requests

Under Tools → Access requests anyone can ask for access, with a reason and for how long (1 hour to 30 days):

  • More rights in a team vault you are in (also with the key button in the vault's title): every manager of the vault may approve. The rights go up at once and come back by themselves at the end.
  • Access to a vault you are not in: you ask a person — a manager or an administrator. That person chooses the vault and clicks Give access; the browser confirms your key's fingerprint and gives the access until the end, then it ends by itself and the vault gets a new key.

Whoever may decide gets an e-mail (when the server sends e-mail) and a number next to Access requests; the person who asked hears of the decision the same way. Requests nobody decides end after 7 days. Requests, decisions and the end of temporary rights are in the audit log. A manager who sets a person's rights by hand ends the temporary rights.

Leaving and a new key

When someone leaves a vault — removed, the access ended, left a group, or the account deleted — they still know the old vault key. PassZit therefore gives the vault a new key: when a manager removes a person in the browser it happens at once; otherwise the vault is marked and the next manager who opens it sees Replace the key now. The browser seals every item again with the new key and the new key to every remaining member. What the person saw before cannot be taken back — change important passwords if needed.

Groups

Administrators make groups under Groups and choose their members. A manager gives a group access to a vault in its settings and ticks what the group may do (the same check boxes as for a person). Because the server cannot seal keys, a manager's browser gives the access: right away when the group is added, and later automatically when a manager opens the vault (new members of the group get access then). Leaving a group ends its access at once. Groups can follow the company directory or the identity provider (Company sign-in).
