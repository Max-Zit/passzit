Shared vaults and groups

Besides the personal vault, PassZit has shared vaults for teams: the router passwords for the IT team, the social media accounts for marketing. Sharing keeps the end-to-end encryption — the server never gets the key of a shared vault.

Making a shared vault

In the vault, the + next to Vaults makes a new shared vault; you manage it. The gear next to its name opens Members and settings. Items are created in the vault shown on the left, or chosen in the item editor under Vault; changing that field moves an existing item (with its files) to another vault.

The policy can allow only administrators to make shared vaults (Settings → Vault policy → Sharing and export).

Rights

RightMay
Usefill and copy logins (web vault and extension), but not show passwords, notes and files
Readsee everything
Read and changealso add, change and delete items
Managealso members, groups, the name and the vault itself

The server refuses changes without the right. Use is a convenience, not a lock: whoever can fill a password into a page can find it out with some effort. A vault always keeps at least one manager.

Adding people

Under Add a person choose the user and the right, optionally an end date. Your browser seals the vault key to the person's public key; the server only stores the sealed key.

The first time, PassZit shows the fingerprint of that key. The person sees the same fingerprint under My account → Your key for sharing; comparing it once (in person or by phone) makes sure nobody put another key in between. PassZit remembers confirmed keys (sealed with your account key) and warns when a person's key changes.

Time-limited access

With an end date the access ends by itself at the end of that day, for example for an external technician. The audit log records the end.

Leaving and a new key

When someone leaves a vault — removed, the access ended, left a group, or the account deleted — they still know the old vault key. PassZit therefore gives the vault a new key: when a manager removes a person in the browser it happens at once; otherwise the vault is marked and the next manager who opens it sees Replace the key now. The browser seals every item again with the new key and the new key to every remaining member. What the person saw before cannot be taken back — change important passwords if needed.

Groups

Administrators make groups under Groups and choose their members. A manager gives a group access to a vault in its settings. Because the server cannot seal keys, a manager's browser gives the access: right away when the group is added, and later automatically when a manager opens the vault (new members of the group get access then). Leaving a group ends its access at once. Groups can follow the company directory or the identity provider (Company sign-in).
